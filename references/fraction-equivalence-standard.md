# Fraction equivalence representation standard

When a lesson teaches equivalence by converting a fraction to tenths or
hundredths, the representation must show the transformation—not merely the
finished decimal grid.

For each modelled example:

1. show the original whole and its original equal partitions;
2. identify the multiplicative factor applied to the denominator;
3. repartition **every** original part by that factor;
4. show that the number of selected parts changes by the same factor;
5. connect the invariant amount to the equivalent fraction and decimal.

For example, a correct model of three fifths as hundredths makes the subdivision
from fifths to hundredths visible and connects
`3/5 = (3 × 20)/(5 × 20) = 60/100 = 0.60`. On projected slides, render fractions
with stacked numerator, vinculum and denominator; the slash form above is for
plain-text metadata only.

A pre-shaded hundred grid labelled with the answer is confirmation, not an
explanation of equivalence. It cannot be the only representation used to teach
the conversion.

The Year 4 pathway may build and name equivalent fractions with concrete or
pictorial partitioning. The Year 5 pathway may connect equivalence to tenths,
hundredths and decimal notation. Both pathways must work on the same central
idea without requiring Year 4 students to have mastered an inactive Year 5
curriculum outcome.

Fail the representation if partitions are unequal, the selected area changes,
only selected parts are subdivided, the multiplication relationship is hidden,
or the diagram, language and equation do not correspond.
