## Visual grammar to preserve

Use a 16:9 canvas with a full-height role-coloured rail at the far left. Use
Trebuchet MS as the dominant projected typeface.

| Role | Pale panel | Strong accent | Use |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Reminder / teacher model | `#FFF3BF` | `#D6A900` | rule, method, worked teacher thinking |
| Question / student task | `#EAF2F8` | `#005A9C` | prompt, reading question, guided or independent task |
| Answer / successful model | `#EAF7EE` | `#1B7F3A` | answer, complete model, success evidence |
| Neutral support | `#FFFFFF` | `#103A5E` or `#CBD5E1` | concise cue, evidence, definition or secondary direction |

Every slide needs a compact uppercase eyebrow identifying the component and
role, followed by a larger plain-language title. Keep the main instructional
panel visually dominant. Use smaller secondary panels only for genuinely
subordinate cues, evidence, explanations or reasoning.

Use the available canvas. Avoid tiny text floating inside decorative boxes,
excessive blank space, generic repeated navy panels, dashboard-like card grids
and a different colour system for each component.

## Component adaptation

- Morning Work: use a whole-canvas working layout with two broad Maths/Literacy
  areas where both are present. Every response must be executable in a book.
- Literacy warm-up: use amber reminder, blue question and green answer triads.
- Shared Reading: use blue paragraph-and-question slides followed immediately
  by green answer slides.
- Writing: use amber for explicit teaching/model decisions, blue for student
  application and green for successful models or answers.
- Numeracy warm-up: preserve the required All / Most / Some body and green Why
  panel. Use blue for prompts and green for matched answers; never colour tiers
  as traffic lights.
- Main Mathematics: use amber for teacher models, blue for student tasks and
  green for answers or completed models while preserving exact notation.
