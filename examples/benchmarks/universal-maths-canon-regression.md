# Universal Mathematics Canon Regression Benchmark

## Purpose

Use this benchmark whenever a change affects the main Mathematics lesson, Mathematics warm-up, mathematical visuals, student-facing mathematical language, question/answer architecture or Mathematics QA.

It tests whether the Daily Lesson Pack applies the same instructional quality principles across number, algebra, measurement, geometry, statistics and probability rather than treating them as fraction-only rules.

## Reusable strengths to preserve

The reviewed fraction presentation demonstrated several useful patterns that should generalise:

- explicit links between mathematical language, visual representations and equations;
- use of more than one representation when each reveals different structure;
- a recognisable model/guided/independent progression;
- misconception analysis;
- separate question and answer slides;
- contextual application;
- a final reasoning task.

The regression benchmark does not approve that presentation as a complete lesson model. It also guards against its documented weaknesses: over-broad scope, completed answers on `We do` slides, mathematically unsafe photographs, procedural shorthand, inconsistent simplification expectations and independent work that exceeds the modelled demand.

## Required cross-topic cases

### Case 1 — Place value and decimals

**Target:** Compare `0.35` and `0.8`.

**PASS:** A place-value chart, hundred-grid or accurately scaled number line shows that eight tenths is greater than thirty-five hundredths. The language names tenths and hundredths, and the symbols match the model.

**FAIL:** The slide relies only on the rule `add a zero` or presents `0.35 > 0.8 because 35 > 8` as an obviously silly distractor without explaining the place-value misconception.

### Case 2 — Multiplication

**Target:** Interpret `4 × 6`.

**PASS:** The array or equal-groups model contains the stated rows/groups and items, and the explanation identifies how the model corresponds to the factors and product.

**FAIL:** The model contains an incorrect number of objects, or a decorative multiplication image appears beside the equation without a mathematical correspondence.

### Case 3 — Division

**Target:** Distinguish sharing and grouping interpretations.

**PASS:** The lesson identifies the interpretation being used, models it accurately and connects the model to the division equation and inverse multiplication fact.

**FAIL:** The lesson teaches division only as `share equally` and then independently asks how many groups can be made without modelling grouping division.

### Case 4 — Area and perimeter

**Target:** Explain why equal perimeter does not guarantee equal area.

**PASS:** Accurate grid diagrams show two shapes with the same perimeter and different areas. The answer addresses both measurements and uses square units for area.

**FAIL:** The answer gives only a formula, uses a photograph whose dimensions cannot be verified, or treats area and perimeter as interchangeable.

### Case 5 — Geometry

**Target:** Compare angle size.

**PASS:** Accurate diagrams vary arm length while preserving angle size, or vary angle size while controlling irrelevant features. The language refers to the amount of turn.

**FAIL:** The visual unintentionally makes the angle with longer arms appear larger and then uses the picture as evidence.

### Case 6 — Algebra and equivalence

**Target:** Solve a simple equation while maintaining equality.

**PASS:** A balance or symbolic model applies the same operation to both sides and explains why equality is preserved.

**FAIL:** The primary explanation is `move it across and change the sign` with no equivalence reasoning.

### Case 7 — Statistics

**Target:** Interpret a graph.

**PASS:** Axes, scale, categories and values are accurate. The question can be answered from the displayed data, and the answer states the evidence used.

**FAIL:** The graph uses unequal or misleading intervals, omits a necessary axis label, or asks students to infer data that are not present.

### Case 8 — Probability

**Target:** Determine probabilities from a sample space.

**PASS:** The complete sample space is represented and outcomes are weighted correctly. The equation and language match the listed outcomes.

**FAIL:** Outcomes are missing, duplicated or treated as equally likely when they are not.

### Case 9 — Guided-practice integrity

**Target:** Any new or fragile concept.

**PASS:** The `We do` question is unresolved on the question slide. The matched answer slide then reveals the complete model and reasoning.

**FAIL:** The question slide already displays the answer, final landing point, completed model or all steps.

### Case 10 — Independent-demand alignment

**Target:** Any procedure-development lesson.

**PASS:** The hardest independent task matches what was modelled in concept, operation, representation, number type, steps, language and reasoning demand.

**FAIL:** The lesson models a one-step or single-operation example and then independently introduces a new operation, representation or multi-step demand.

### Case 11 — Reasoning and problem-solving labels

**PASS:** A reasoning task requires comparison, justification, generalisation or evaluation. A problem-solving task requires students to choose or construct a pathway.

**FAIL:** A routine calculation is labelled reasoning because it asks for working, or labelled problem solving solely because it has a context sentence.

### Case 12 — Question/answer completion

**PASS:** If the prompt asks students to draw, label, explain, compare, justify, prove or write an equation, the answer models every requested element.

**FAIL:** The answer gives only the final number or verbal statement when the prompt requires a representation or justification.

## Representative generation prompts

Use at least two prompts from different concept families after a Mathematics-canon change:

1. `Year 4 concept-introduction lesson: interpret multiplication as equal groups and arrays.`
2. `Year 5 procedure-development lesson: add decimals using place value, including regrouping.`
3. `Year 5 reasoning lesson: compare area and perimeter using rectilinear shapes.`
4. `Year 4 concept-introduction lesson: classify and compare angles by amount of turn.`
5. `Year 5 statistics lesson: interpret a column graph with a non-unit scale.`
6. `Year 5 probability lesson: construct and use a complete sample space.`

## Required review record

For each tested output, record:

- lesson mode;
- central new idea;
- prerequisite retrieval;
- representation choice and purpose;
- exactness check;
- representation/language/notation correspondence;
- guided-practice integrity;
- hardest independent demand and its modelled precursor;
- answer-completion check;
- rendered readability result;
- final `PASS` or `FAIL`.

A change fails regression if it improves one concept family while breaking these universal requirements in another.
