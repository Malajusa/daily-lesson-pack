# Mathematics demand and responsive progression regression

Use when changing Numeracy calibration, main Mathematics progression, review or packaging.
These are sanitised mathematical cases, not a record of student attainment.

## Captured classroom failure

A supplied daily deck used Most `6 × 8` and Some `48 ÷ 6` on slide 6.
Its final two graph pairs asked students to read, subtract and total very small counts.
Copies of slides 2–11 passed the existing structural validator. This was not
pedagogical approval. Slides 16–21 repeatedly used an L outline; notes allocated
14 minutes to recording the two partitions already explored. The teacher reported
that students found the intended partitions immediately and later tasks lost
their purpose. No generator/reviewer execution provenance or class-wide mastery
claim follows from that report. Do not distribute the original classroom deck.

The maintained weekly direction already allowed complex figures, alternative
partitions and unfamiliar composing/decomposing problems. Keep that progression.

## Responsive lesson cases

| Case | Observation | Required route |
| --- | --- | --- |
| R1 | Independent responses show the opening partitions and a reason they fill the outline | Skip already demonstrated models/repetition; use the prepared unfamiliar/constraint task with answers and hints available. |
| R2 | A few vocal students answer immediately; others' independent responses are mixed | Collect independent evidence, then run concurrent support and advance tasks; no whole-class mastery inference from speed or volume. |
| R3 | Lines do not reach boundaries or pieces are misidentified; later work succeeds with support | Model the identified difficulty and independently recheck a fresh outline before fading support. |

Do not impose fixed support/core/challenge membership. Durations are estimates
attached to real tasks and change with evidence; they do not manufacture demand.

## Teaching rubric

Judge actual prompts, diagrams, answers and routes, not their headings.

1. All is accessible using resolved prior learning; Most is worthwhile secure retrieval.
2. Some adds a specific same-focus demand without an untaught procedure merely for difficulty.
3. Tiers remain separate complete tasks and do not reveal one another's answers.
4. Every substantial lesson task adds demand or has a justified practice purpose.
5. Opening evidence can show that the planned teaching is already understood.
6. Support, normal-teaching and advance routes name executable next tasks and skip points.
7. Enough mathematics is prepared for the allotted block, including R1, with exact
   teacher solutions/valid-solution criteria, hints and an aligned fresh exit.
8. Independent transfer is allowed using established concepts; new concepts are taught.

Record each criterion as PASS/FAIL with specific evidence and repair. Automated
tests verify contracts/review coverage, never educational quality or classroom effect.
Missing independent execution or host playback remains unverified.

## Candidate mathematical examples

These are authoring/review fixtures, not teacher-approved difficulty or curriculum alignment.
Keep the class's resolved taught knowledge as the authority when adapting them.

For a stepped outline, use vertices (0,0), (8,0), (8,2), (5,2),
(5,4), (3,4), (3,6), (0,6). Horizontal decomposition has rectangles
8×2, 5×2 and 3×2. Vertical decomposition has 3×6, 2×4 and 3×2.
Each fills the same outline with no overlap. Two rectangles cannot suffice:
their shared internal boundary would be one straight segment, which cannot
resolve both non-aligned inward corners. That argument is connected extension,
not a prerequisite for the core task. Numerical area calculations are optional
only when authorised taught content supports them.

Use a second concept family (for example addition strategies) when reviewing a
change; successful shape examples alone do not establish general improvement.

### Candidate warm-up set

Each response below is one direct answer. Use only where the relevant knowledge
is taught; these fixtures do not place real learners.

| Pair/focus | All | Most | Some | Answers: All / Most / Some | Added demand |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 Addition | 240 + 160 | 368 + 257 | 398 + □ = 1,045 | 400 / 625 / 647 | Recover an unknown addend across place values. |
| 2 Subtraction | 600 − 250 | 802 − 367 | □ − 275 = 648 | 350 / 435 / 923 | Identify the unknown starting amount rather than execute the displayed subtraction. |
| 3 Multiplication | 6 × 4 | 14 × 6 | □ × 6 = 252 | 24 / 84 / 42 | Find a larger unknown factor using an independently chosen inverse/decomposition. |
| 4 Graph, counts 75/125/100 | Read B | B − A | Total A/B/C/D = 400; find D | 125 / 50 / 100 | Infer an unshown category using the stated total and all known values. |
| 5 Graph, counts 40/60/90 | Read Green | Green − Blue | Move 30 counters from Green's container to Red's; which team now has most? | 90 / 30 / Red (70) | Update two quantities and compare the resulting distribution. |

Pair 4 uses categories A=75, B=125, C=100 and unknown D, with a visible
25-unit scale and total 400. Pair 5 uses team containers Red=40, Blue=60, Green=90,
with a visible 10-unit scale. Include complete scales/keys on each question,
not only in these notes. Every task is answerable independently of another
tier's result. Equal answers can occur coincidentally; that is not by itself
answer leakage. Briefing rows must still justify the actual demand.

Representation check: transferring counters between team containers changes
their counts. Moving pears into an apple group does not turn them into apples;
that earlier candidate story was rejected during implementation review. Keep
the mathematical quantities consistent with the category meanings.

### Candidate shape route table

| Route | Evidence and teacher action | Task destination / skip |
| --- | --- | --- |
| R1 | Independent partitions plus a valid unchanged-outline explanation | Move from shape.open to shape.transfer; skip shape.model and shape.guided. |
| R2 | Independently collected work is mixed | Teacher supports shape.model/guided while ready learners use shape.transfer, then shape.challenge with prepared hints. |
| R3 | Incomplete lines or incorrect shape names | Model the difficulty; use shape.recheck on a fresh outline, then guided or transfer work according to that independent response. |

Task IDs must resolve to actual final slide numbers in the delivered briefing.
Use the stepped outline above for shape.transfer: two three-rectangle
partitions, then compare which lines changed. Reserve shape.challenge for the
two-rectangle impossibility question, followed by creating a different stepped
outline with two non-aligned inward corners and testing the same reasoning.
Hints: locate inward corners; try horizontal partitions; try vertical
partitions; consider the single boundary shared by two rectangles.
Check solutions against the unchanged outline, piece properties and no gaps or
overlaps. A new outline for shape.exit checks independent partitioning/naming;
do not display its solution before collecting work.

For a 40-minute synthetic block, budget up to 3 minutes for opening evidence,
up to 10 for targeted teaching/recheck, 20–25 for transfer/comparison/challenge
and 5 for exit/reflection. R1 starts the substantial tasks early. These are
candidate estimates, not a requirement to stretch an already solved task;
shorten completed work and use the prepared creation/invariant investigation.

## Bounded implementation evidence — 15 October 2026

A fresh independent reviewer examined five baseline and five revised synthetic
consumers of the same supplied Year 4/5 context, plus an addition-strategy lesson.
Raw complete warm-up content acceptance was 0/5 baseline versus 4/5 revised. The
fifth revised consumer repeated the fruit-category error above; its separate
repair passed content review without rewriting the raw failure. Authored sustained
R1/R2/R3 shape progression met the full criterion in 0/5 baseline versus 5/5 revised
consumers. These counts describe bounded task content, not classroom effects.

Final sample assembly uses canonical sources and the supported renderer. All three
teacher decks and both student-card decks pass typography and containment; the
five-pair warm-up passes standalone structure. Independent asset/text review
checked complete answers and actual route destinations across shapes and addition.
Full-slide rendering is blocked by host LibreOffice DeploymentException and
PowerPoint playback remains unverified. The source/package candidate is unreleased;
actual classroom pitch and pace still require ordinary teaching feedback. The
execution report records exact source, hashes, repairs and remaining evidence.
