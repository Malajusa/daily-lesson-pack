# Independent interactive Mathematics review cases

Give a fresh reviewer the cases, not the acceptance notes. Ask them to infer the
goal, inspect correspondence, identify any defect and cite the exact state.
Use actual rendered cases for visual judgements; prose-only answers cannot
certify recognisability or margins. Record actual execution and source hashes.

## Cases

A. Goal: add by making ten. Problem 8 + 5. Text says split 5 into 2 and 3;
diagram shows an undivided bar labelled 13. Equation 8 + 5 = 13.

B. Goal: convert masses. A level balance shows one 1 kg bag and two 500 g bags,
with a plus between them. Text: same mass, different units. Equation 1 kg = 1000 g.
The next example converts 0.5 kg to 500 g and explains half a kilogram.

C. First visible content is 17 + 8 = 25. Only below the completed working does
the learner encounter 17 + 8 = ?. The explanation and diagram are accurate.

D. Goal: g and kg. A well-drawn balance and correct equation are present.
The exit check asks only whether a depicted object is a bag or a box.

E. Goal: count each object once and tell how many. Six visible objects accompany
the numeral 6. No arithmetic equation is shown.

F. Goal: convert mass. A bag label remains inside the SVG rectangle but crosses
the fold of the bag and has no side padding on its illustrated front face.

G. Goal: find a missing part. The model demonstrates 10 = 3 + 7; the guided
question asks 10 = 4 + ? but shows a completed bar labelled 4 and 6.

## Acceptance notes (withhold during blind review)

A fails correspondence: the described split and recombination are absent.
B is a valid conversion model; reject a blanket ban on half/fractions/bars.
C fails problem-first presentation even though the final mathematics is correct.
D fails authored goal and exit alignment. Diagram presence cannot compensate.
E is valid numeral/quantity correspondence; do not force a pointless equation.
F fails semantic containment; require rendered inspection of the bag front face.
G leaks the guided answer. Independent and transfer stages may omit scaffolds.
