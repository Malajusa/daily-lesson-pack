# DLP Mathematics Lesson

## Ownership

Own the main Mathematics lesson. Do not absorb the cumulative numeracy warm-up into this sequence.

## Topic integrity

Identify the actual mathematical concept before choosing a representation. A number line, fraction wall or diagram may support the topic but must not silently become the lesson topic.

For overview-controlled days, the weekly Mathematics topic/code and the day-level focus must agree with the authoritative Year 4/5 overview.

## Mathematics Slide Clarity Standard

Every student-facing mathematics task must communicate:
- the **action** students take;
- the **mathematical focus**;
- any required **representation/resource**;
- the expected **student output**.

Each slide should stand alone well enough that a relief teacher and an average student can determine what is required.

Avoid vague conversational shorthand. Do not use phrases such as `Does it settle it?`, `useful form`, `efficient proof` or similar wording that requires students to infer the mathematical decision. State the decision literally.

## Teaching sequence

Keep retrieval, readiness and prerequisite teaching distinct.

When the concept is new or fragile:
1. readiness/prerequisite check;
2. complete teacher model from start to finish;
3. `We do` / guided example;
4. guided practice;
5. independent application;
6. review/exit check that measures the intended concept.

Do not treat a token warm-up as prerequisite teaching.

## Mathematical visuals

Before drawing, define what the visual must prove or show. Verify calculations, labels, scales, equal partitions and coordinates. Task slides must not reveal the answer. Answer slides must model the requested strategy and place the primary answer in bold/visually dominant form.

For fraction comparison, explicitly teach strategy selection where relevant: same denominator, same numerator, equivalence, benchmark to one-half, distance from one, and mixed/improper representation. Explain when a method is inconclusive.

## Differentiation

Differentiate through representation, reasoning and abstraction, not only by assigning more questions.

## QA

Fail if instructions are ambiguous, the representation changes the mathematical focus, a diagram is mathematically inaccurate, a task slide reveals the answer, the answer does not model the requested method, or the exit check measures only the representation rather than the concept.
