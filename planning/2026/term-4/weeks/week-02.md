- Deepen the Term 4 foundations and explicitly teach concepts students

  available; no major summative assessment.

## English — Writing

**Narrative: character motivation, complication and sentence craft**

**Lesson 1 — Characters need something**

**Focus:** Character goals and motivations

**Key teaching points**
- A character’s goal gives the narrative direction.
- Motivation explains why the character acts.
- A useful complication interferes with something the character wants or needs.
- Character decisions should make sense based on what the reader knows about them.

**Student task/output**
- Analyse a model character: what do they want, why, and what might stop them?
- Develop a character with a goal, motivation and relevant strength or flaw.

**Lesson 2 — Building an effective complication**

**Focus:** Problems that actually affect the narrative

**Key teaching points**
- A complication should disrupt the character’s situation.
- Strong complications create decisions or consequences.
- Random events are weaker when they have no connection to the character or established situation.
- The complication does not need to be enormous to create tension.

**Student task/output**
- Given several orientations, devise complications that connect logically, interfere with the character’s goal and create possibilities for later events.

**Lesson 3 — Show rather than explain everything**

**Focus:** Revealing emotion and character through evidence

**Key teaching points**
- Use actions, body language, dialogue, thoughts and reactions.
- Do not turn “show, don’t tell” into a rigid rule that emotions can never be named.

**Student task/output**
- Revise simple statements such as “Alex was nervous” into short passages that give evidence of nervousness.

**Lesson 4 — Sentence craft for narrative**

**Focus:** Sentence choices that support meaning and pace

**Key teaching points**
- Revisit simple, compound and complex sentences.
- Vary sentence openings.
- Use shorter sentences deliberately for impact.
- Avoid repetitive sentence structures and make purposeful conjunction choices.

**Student task/output**
- Improve a deliberately repetitive narrative passage and explain at least two changes.

**Lesson 5 — Orientation to complication**

**Focus:** Combining Week 1 and Week 2 learning

**Student task/output**
- Produce a short narrative section with an established character/setting, a clear goal or motivation, an effective complication and a believable reaction.

## English — Reading

**Character, inference and narrative development**

**Lesson 1 — Inferring character traits**
- Use actions, dialogue, thoughts, reactions and other characters’ responses as evidence.
- Infer traits and identify textual evidence.

**Lesson 2 — Motivation and cause and effect**
- Track character wants → character acts → consequence occurs.
- Explain connections rather than merely recounting events.

**Lesson 3 — How complications change a story**
- Identify the initial situation, complication, what changes and new decisions available to the character.

**Lesson 4 — Vocabulary precision**
- Compare near-synonyms with different strength or connotation.
- Choose words for intended meaning rather than novelty.
- Select precise vocabulary in context and justify choices.

**Lesson 5 — Author choice and tension**
- Examine withholding information, sentence length, paragraph breaks, character reactions, setting detail and pacing.
- Explain one or two author choices and their effect on the reader.

## Mathematics

**Symmetry and angles**

**Lesson 1 — Line symmetry**
- Identify lines of symmetry, distinguish symmetrical/non-symmetrical figures and test rather than rely on visual judgement.
- Recognise zero, one or multiple lines of symmetry.
- Resources: mirrors, folding/cut-outs and grid paper.

**Lesson 2 — Creating symmetrical figures**
- Complete half-drawn figures, reflect points/shapes and create own symmetrical designs.
- Justify the position of corresponding points.

**Lesson 3 — Rotational symmetry**
- Investigate whether a shape maps onto itself during a turn and the order of rotational symmetry.
- Compare shapes with line symmetry, rotational symmetry, both or neither.
- Use physical rotation/tracing resources.

**Lesson 4 — Understanding and classifying angles**
- Use a right angle as the benchmark.
- Classify acute, right, obtuse and straight angles.
- Arm length and orientation do not determine angle size.

**Lesson 5 — Angles in shapes and the environment**
- Identify and reason about angles in 2D shapes, composite figures, letters/patterns and environmental examples.
- Require justification rather than labels only.

## HASS

**Resources, scarcity and choice**

**Lesson 1 — What are resources?**
- Build from needs and wants.
- Introduce natural, human and capital resources at an age-appropriate level.
- Given a familiar product/service, identify resources needed to provide it.

**Lesson 2 — Why limited resources create choices**
- Scarcity means there is not always enough time, money, materials or other resources to do or have everything people want.
- For each scenario identify what is limited, available choices, preferred choice, what is given up and why different people may choose differently.

## Health

**Hand and oral hygiene**

**Lesson 1 — Hand hygiene**
- Hands can transfer microorganisms and contaminants.
- Teach situations where handwashing is particularly important, effective technique, soap/drying and why a very quick rinse is less effective.
- Avoid framing all microorganisms as harmful.
- Analyse scenarios and decide whether handwashing is needed, why and what risk it reduces.
- Practical demonstration materials if facilities allow.

**Lesson 2 — Oral hygiene**
- Explain regular care, effective brushing, fluoride toothpaste at an age-appropriate level, cleaning between teeth, food/drink habits and regular dental care.
- Evaluate example oral-health routines and identify strengths and improvements.

## Digital Technologies

**Data representation and privacy**

**Lesson 1 — Digital systems use data**
- Explore text, numbers, images, sound and user selections/responses as data.
- Different kinds of information can be represented in different ways.
- Identify input data, what a system does with it and the resulting output for familiar systems.

**Lesson 2 — Personal information and privacy**
- Distinguish information suitable to share publicly, information that should remain private and information requiring adult guidance.
- Discuss personal details, usernames, passwords, photos, location and school information.
- Privacy depends on context; once shared digitally, control may become more difficult.
- Analyse digital scenarios and justify a safe response.

**Optional Lesson 3 — Representing the same information differently**
- Compare words, numbers, symbols, images, tables or simple coded representations of the same information.
