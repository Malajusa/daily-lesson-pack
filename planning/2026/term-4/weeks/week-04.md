## English — Writing

**Narrative cohesion, paragraphing and planning**

**Lesson 1 — Paragraphs organise narrative events**
- Paragraphs are not a fixed number of sentences.
- A new paragraph can signal change in time, place, speaker, action or focus.
- Paragraph breaks help the reader follow the story.
- Improve an un-paragraphed narrative extract and explain selected paragraph-break choices.

**Lesson 2 — Making events connect**
- Use clear pronoun reference, useful time connectives, repeated/key ideas, cause-and-effect links and logical transitions.
- Cohesion is more than adding “then/next/after that”.
- Repair a deliberately disjointed narrative passage so the sequence becomes clear.

**Lesson 3 — Planning a complete narrative**
- Plan character/motivation, setting, complication, escalating events, climax and resolution.
- A plan describes story logic rather than pre-writing every sentence.
- Produce a concise practice narrative plan for use in Week 5.

## English — Reading

**Cohesion, sequence and author organisation**

**Lesson 1 — Tracking a narrative across paragraphs**
- Identify what changes from paragraph to paragraph, why the author may start a new paragraph and how each section contributes to the developing story.

**Lesson 2 — Cohesive devices in context**
- Locate pronouns, repeated concepts, conjunctions, time references and cause/effect links.
- Explain what helps connect a sentence to the one before it.

**Lesson 3 — Summarising the important events**
- Distinguish major events, supporting detail and incidental detail.
- Write a brief summary showing the causal sequence rather than sentence-by-sentence retell.

## Mathematics

**Location, grid references and geometry consolidation**

**Lesson 1 — Reading grid references**
- Interpret labelled grids, locate objects/points, give grid references and distinguish horizontal/vertical movement.
- Use maps, game boards and familiar visual contexts.

**Lesson 2 — Following and giving location instructions**
- Follow sequences of grid movements, describe routes, identify errors and create directions for another student.
- Instructions must be precise enough to reproduce the same result.

**Lesson 3 — Geometry retrieval challenge**
- Combine 2D properties, symmetry, angles and location.
- Place shapes at specified locations, identify angles in grid designs, complete symmetrical grid patterns and describe properties.

## HASS

**Choices, alternatives and consequences**

**Lesson 1 — Every choice has an alternative**
- Use scenarios involving limited money, land, time or school budget.
- Identify the limited resource, alternatives, choice, what is given up and possible consequences.
- Introduce trade-off in accessible language.
- Respond: “When we choose one option, what might we be giving up?”

## Health

**Body hygiene and changing hygiene needs**

**Lesson 1 — Body hygiene**
- Teach regular washing, skin/hair/nail care, clean clothing and managing sweat/body odour.
- Hygiene routines vary with activity, weather, individual needs and changes in the body.
- Evaluate fictional daily routines and recommend sensible improvements.

**Lesson 2 — Changing bodies and changing routines**
- Discuss increased sweating, body odour, oilier skin/hair, changing clothing needs, deodorant/antiperspirant and independent routines.
- Keep the focus on practical hygiene rather than expanding into a full puberty unit.
- Create a practical hygiene checklist for a fictional student whose activity/body-care needs are changing.

## Digital Technologies

**Algorithms and privacy consolidation**

**Lesson 1 — Instructions must be precise**
- Introduce an algorithm as an ordered sequence of instructions designed to achieve a result.
- Use unplugged grid movement, scrambled steps, missing steps and debugging.
- Computers follow instructions literally.

**Optional Lesson 2 — Privacy decisions revisited**
- Analyse who is requesting information, what information is requested, whether sharing is appropriate and what safer action could be taken.
