## English — Writing

**Narrative: tension, pacing, climax and resolution**

**Lesson 1 — Building tension**
- Tension increases when problems become more difficult or uncertain.
- Writers can control what information the reader receives.
- Character reactions communicate seriousness.
- Tension can arise from uncertainty, conflict and difficult choices — not only danger.
- Take a simple complication and develop two or three escalating events that logically increase difficulty.

**Lesson 2 — Controlling pace**
- Use sentence length, paragraph length, detail, action, dialogue and internal thought.
- Rewrite a rushed scene so an important moment receives greater emphasis.

**Lesson 3 — Dialogue that does something**
- Dialogue should reveal character, advance events, create tension or provide necessary information.
- Revisit dialogue punctuation, new speaker/new paragraph and sensible dialogue tags.
- Write a short exchange where dialogue changes what happens next.

**Lesson 4 — Climax and resolution**
- Climax is the point of greatest significance/conflict/decision; resolution shows what happens because of it.
- Avoid sudden new solutions, unexplained coincidence and abrupt endings.
- Given incomplete narratives, propose logical climaxes and resolutions.

**Lesson 5 — Complete narrative practice**
- Plan and write a shorter practice narrative with orientation, motivation, complication, rising tension, climax, resolution, paragraphing and cohesion.
- Use feedback to target one or two high-value improvements per student.

## English — Reading

**Tension, pacing and author craft**

**Lesson 1 — How authors build tension**
- Identify information revealed/withheld, setting, character reaction, sentence construction and pacing.
- Explain with evidence how tension develops.

**Lesson 2 — Pacing**
- Compare fast-moving and deliberately slowed sections.
- Identify what the author changes and the effect.

**Lesson 3 — Dialogue and character**
- Analyse what dialogue reveals about relationships, motivation, conflict and personality.

**Lesson 4 — Climax and resolution**
- Locate the climax and justify why it is the climax.
- Explain how earlier events prepared for it and what the resolution does.

**Lesson 5 — Integrated narrative analysis**
- Use a short complete narrative covering motivation, inference, vocabulary, cohesion, tension and author choice.
- Complete a small set of deeper reading questions.

## Mathematics

**3D objects, nets and geometry consolidation**

**Lesson 1 — Properties of 3D objects**
- Revisit faces, edges, vertices, curved surfaces and relationships between 2D faces and 3D objects.
- Classify based on properties rather than appearance.
- Sort and justify classifications.

**Lesson 2 — From 3D objects to nets**
- Unfold models, match nets to objects, identify face shapes and predict connections.
- Resources: physical 3D models and printed nets.

**Lesson 3 — Which nets work?**
- A collection of correct faces does not automatically make a valid net; arrangement matters.
- Predict, test and revise reasoning about valid/invalid nets.

**Lesson 4 — Designing nets**
- Complete missing faces, draw known nets and compare multiple valid nets.
- Resources: scissors/tape or equivalent.

**Lesson 5 — Geometry consolidation**
- Bring together 2D shapes, symmetry, angles, grid references/location, 3D objects and nets.
- Solve mixed reasoning problems to identify gaps before assessment.

## HASS

**Scarcity, alternative uses and trade-offs**

**Lesson 1 — Resources have alternative uses**
- Explore how money, land, time, workers and materials can be allocated differently.
- Identify limited resource, possible uses, chosen allocation, justification and what can no longer be funded/done.

**Lesson 2 — Decisions have consequences**
- Consider who benefits, who misses out, short/long-term consequences and why different people may prefer different options.
- Economic choices often involve trade-offs rather than one obviously correct answer.

**Lesson 3 — Bringing the unit together**
- Return to the central inquiry question and integrate needs/wants, resources, scarcity, choices, alternatives and trade-offs.
- Write a structured explanation using a concrete example.

## Health

**Food hygiene and respiratory hygiene**

**Lesson 1 — Food hygiene**
- Teach clean hands, clean preparation surfaces, avoiding cross-contamination, appropriate storage, recognising unsafe food and responsibility when preparing food for others.
- Analyse food-preparation scenarios, identify risks and recommend actions.

**Lesson 2 — Respiratory hygiene**
- Teach covering coughs/sneezes, tissues, hand hygiene, sensible sharing behaviours and actions when unwell.
- Avoid implying hygiene can guarantee illness prevention.
- Create/evaluate a practical routine for reducing spread in a shared environment.

**Lesson 3 — Bringing hygiene together**
- Use mixed scenarios spanning hand, oral, body, swimming/shared spaces, food and respiratory hygiene.
- Answer “What should this person do, and why?”

## Digital Technologies

**Algorithms to digital solutions**

**Lesson 1 — Sequencing and debugging**
- Follow algorithms, identify missing/misplaced instructions, correct errors and explain why sequence matters.

**Lesson 2 — User input changes what happens**
- Explore clicks, key presses, choices and entered values as inputs.
- Use “If the user does X, the system does Y”.

**Lesson 3 — Plan a simple digital solution**
- Identify purpose, user, input, output and main sequence of instructions.
- Keep scope small; integration with another learning area is optional.
- Plan an interactive quiz, branching story, basic maths game or similar small product.

**Lesson 4 — Begin building**
- Make the core function work first and test frequently.
- Do not let visual decoration consume development time.
