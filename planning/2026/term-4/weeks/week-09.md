# Week 9 — 7–11 December 2026

## Weekly purpose

- Deepen post-assessment learning through experimentation, investigation and application.

## English — Writing

**Creative control**

**Lesson 1 — Voice**
- Compare narrators with distinctive voices.
- Rewrite neutral material to create a recognisable narrator.

**Lesson 2 — First-person and third-person choices**
- Explore what each viewpoint allows the reader to know.
- Transform a passage from one perspective to another.

**Lesson 3 — Sustaining mood**
- Use vocabulary, imagery, setting and sentence construction together to sustain mood.

**Lesson 4 — Constraint writing**
- Use a creative constraint such as no dialogue, one location, two characters or a ten-minute story window to force deliberate choices.

**Lesson 5 — Independent creative piece**
- Apply one or more techniques without rubric-driven assessment.

## English — Reading

**Deeper literary response**

**Lesson 1 — Narrator reliability and limited knowledge**

**Lesson 2 — How setting influences events**

**Lesson 3 — Comparing themes across texts**

**Lesson 4 — Language and tone**

**Lesson 5 — Extended response**
- Support discussion or written response with textual evidence.

## Mathematics

**Chance experiments and numerical probability**

**Lesson 1 — Predict then test**
- Predict outcomes, conduct repeated trials and compare predictions with experimental results.

**Lesson 2 — Why results vary**
- Compare group results from the same experiment.
- Random processes do not necessarily produce exact expected proportions in small samples.

**Lesson 3 — Representing probability**
- Connect chance to fractions where appropriate: favourable outcomes / total equally likely outcomes.

**Lesson 4 — Fair and unfair games revisited**
- Test Week 8 games and collect data; judge whether evidence supports fairness predictions.

**Lesson 5 — Improve the game**
- Adjust a game to make it fairer or change the likelihood of a target outcome.

## HASS

**Economic decisions in everyday life**

**Lesson 1 — Individual choices**
- Use practical fictional scenarios involving limited money, time and competing priorities.
- Identify trade-offs/opportunity costs in accessible terms.

**Lesson 2 — Choices affect other people**
- Examine effects on families, businesses, schools and communities.

**Lesson 3 — Decision-making simulation**
- Change circumstances after the initial decision: budget decreases, new need appears or a resource becomes unavailable.
- Revise the allocation and explain why.

## Health

**Advertising and hygiene claims**

**Lesson 1 — Claims versus evidence**
- Use age-appropriate fictional/generic hygiene advertising.
- Identify factual information, persuasive language and claims needing evidence.

**Lesson 2 — Do I need this?**
- Consider hygiene need, existing alternatives, cost and marketing influence.
- Decide whether a fictional person actually needs a product and justify the decision.

## Digital Technologies

**Refine for users**

**Lesson 1 — Usability**
- Observe another user interacting with the solution; record confusion, success and unnecessary steps.

**Lesson 2 — Improve interface/function**
- Make changes based on evidence.

**Lesson 3 — Debugging challenge**
- Diagnose deliberately broken small programs/algorithms.

**Lesson 4 — Final extension build**
- Complete the post-assessment extension.
