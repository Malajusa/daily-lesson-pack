# Week 3 — 26–30 October 2026

## Weekly purpose

- Swimming week 1. Approximately two hours of classroom time is lost
  each day, so curriculum load is deliberately reduced.
- Maintain literacy and numeracy momentum, consolidate Weeks 1–2, use
  short self-contained lessons, exploit swimming as an authentic Health
  context and avoid major projects/assessment.

## English — Writing

**Narrative craft: character reactions, vocabulary and sentence control**

**Lesson 1 — Characters react to complications**
- A complication should cause a response.
- Different characters may respond differently to the same event.
- Reactions should connect to established character.
- Actions, thoughts and dialogue can reveal a reaction.
- Given an orientation and complication, write the character’s immediate response using action, thought and dialogue.

**Lesson 2 — Precise verbs and vocabulary**
- Compare alternatives such as walked/crept/staggered/marched; looked/glanced/stared/peered; grabbed/collected/snatched/lifted.
- Different words change the reader’s picture and interpretation.
- Improve a bland passage by replacing imprecise words and explain why selected replacements fit.

**Lesson 3 — Controlling pace with sentences**
- Short sentences can emphasise sudden action.
- Longer sentences can slow a moment or add detail.
- Repeated structures can create monotony.
- Deliberate variation can control the reader’s experience.
- Revise a brief action scene by varying sentence construction.

## English — Reading

**Short-text comprehension and narrative inference**

**Lesson 1 — Evidence for inference**
- Distinguish a plausible guess from an inference supported by the text.
- Respond to short inference questions with textual evidence.

**Lesson 2 — Vocabulary through context**
- Use surrounding sentences, word parts, synonyms/contrasts and the overall situation.
- Substitute another word and judge whether meaning/tone changes.

**Lesson 3 — Character reaction and consequence**
- Track event → character response → consequence.

## Mathematics

**Geometry consolidation: symmetry and angles**

**Lesson 1 — Symmetry retrieval and application**
- Revisit line symmetry, completing symmetrical figures, possible symmetry lines and rotational symmetry where taught.
- Solve short reasoning tasks such as “Which figure does not belong?”, “Is this symmetrical? Prove it”, and draw a figure meeting symmetry conditions.

**Lesson 2 — Angle retrieval**
- Revisit acute, right, obtuse and straight angles.
- Correct misconceptions about arm length and orientation.
- Classify and justify rather than simply label.

**Lesson 3 — Geometry problem solving**
- Combine 2D properties, symmetry, angles and composite shapes.
- Draw or identify a shape satisfying multiple conditions and explain how you know.

## HASS

**Resources and choices: consolidation**

**Lesson 1 — What happens when a resource is limited?**
- Use short scenarios with limited money, time, materials, space or people.
- Identify the limited resource, choices, influences on the decision and what must be given up.
- Write a short explanation: “Why do limited resources force people to make choices?”

## Health

**Hygiene around swimming and shared facilities**

**Lesson 1 — Hygiene before and after swimming**
- Explore showering, drying thoroughly, changing out of wet clothing, clean clothing, swimwear care and sensible separation of personal belongings.
- Teach reasons behind behaviours rather than lists.

**Lesson 2 — Shared spaces and personal items**
- Discuss towels, drink bottles, clothing, footwear, hairbrushes/personal-care items and shared surfaces.
- Focus on sensible risk reduction without unnecessary fear of germs.
- Analyse swimming-day scenarios and recommend an appropriate hygiene response with a reason.

## Digital Technologies

**Short application: inputs, outputs and data**

**Lesson 1 — Follow the data**
- Revisit input → process/data → output using familiar digital systems.
- Create a simple diagram explaining one example.

**Optional Lesson 2 — Digital privacy scenarios**
- Decide whether information is safe to share, private, for trusted people/services only, or should be checked with an adult.
- Require a reason for each decision.
