# Week 6 — 16–20 November 2026

## Weekly purpose

- Begin the formal assessment window with the Narrative assessment fixed
  this week.
- Protect adequate time and cognitive space for narrative assessment,
  collect geometry evidence where practical, and continue
  HASS/Health/Digital Tech without stacking unnecessary high-stakes
  tasks.

## English — Writing

**Narrative assessment**

**Lesson 1 — Final craft consolidation**
- Revisit orientation, motivation, logical complication, escalating
  events, climax, resolution, paragraphing, sentence control and vocabulary.
- Analyse a short unfamiliar narrative and identify one effective
  feature and one possible improvement.

**Lesson 2 — Narrative assessment**
- Students complete the Term 4 narrative assessment under the school’s
  agreed assessment conditions.
- Do not conference individual story ideas or correct sentences; use
  only permitted supports.

**Lesson 3 — Assessment completion where required**
- Only legitimate completion/absence needs continue.
- Other students complete independent literacy tasks rather than
  receiving extra coaching that changes conditions.

**Lesson 4 — Post-assessment reflection**
- Reflection is not part of the assessment score.
- Identify what was effective, where writing became difficult and one
  feature to improve next time.

**Lesson 5 — Writing for enjoyment**
- Return quickly to writing as something other than assessment.
- Choose a short task such as alternative perspective, alternative
  ending, minor-character development or setting extension.

## English — Reading

**Narrative interpretation and author craft**

**Lesson 1 — Comparing character perspectives**
- Examine how two characters interpret the same event differently using
  evidence, motivation, bias/limited knowledge and perspective.

**Lesson 2 — Theme and message**
- Move beyond plot; distinguish theme from topic, plot summary or
  generic moral.

**Lesson 3 — Evidence-based interpretation**
- Use claim → evidence → explanation.
- Respond to a short text with an evidence-based interpretation.

**Lesson 4 — Author craft**
- Revisit pacing, vocabulary, dialogue, sentence construction and
  withholding information.
- Explain effect rather than merely naming techniques.

**Lesson 5 — Independent reading response**
- Use an unfamiliar narrative extract as normal teaching evidence rather
  than another formal assessment event.

## Mathematics

**Geometry consolidation and assessment evidence**

**Lesson 1 — Mixed geometry retrieval**
- Revisit 2D properties, symmetry, angles, grid references, 3D objects
  and nets; follow retrieval with reasoning.

**Lesson 2 — Geometry reasoning**
- Use multi-concept problems: identify 3D objects from clues, judge
  nets, create shapes meeting conditions or construct figures from coordinates.
- Justify reasoning.

**Lesson 3 — Mathematics assessment**
- Collect principal geometry evidence emphasising application,
  representation and reasoning rather than vocabulary recall.

**Lesson 4 — Assessment completion / targeted teaching**
- Students with complete evidence undertake extension; targeted teaching
  addresses misconceptions without coaching assessment answers.

**Lesson 5 — Rich geometry problem**
- Apply several geometry concepts collaboratively after assessment
  evidence is secured.
- Design an object/structure under 2D, 3D, angle, symmetry and location constraints.

## HASS

**Bringing Economics concepts together**

**Lesson 1 — Economics concept map**
- Connect needs/wants → resources → scarcity → choices → alternatives →
  trade-offs → consequences.
- Use examples rather than isolated definition memorisation.

**Lesson 2 — Applying the concepts**
- Use a realistic allocation scenario.
- Identify scarcity, choices, recommended option, trade-off and likely consequences.

**Lesson 3 — Compare decisions**
- Compare two different reasonable decisions from the same scenario.
- Different choices can be reasonable because people value outcomes differently.

## Health

**Hygiene: application and consolidation**

**Lesson 1 — Hygiene decisions**
- Use mixed scenarios spanning hand, oral, body, swimming/shared
  facilities, food and respiratory hygiene.
- Answer what the person should do and why.

**Lesson 2 — Cause and consequence**
- Connect hygiene behaviours with likely consequences without exaggerated claims.

**Lesson 3 — Designing an effective routine**
- Create a practical routine for a fictional person whose day includes
  school, sport/swimming, meals and shared facilities.
- Justify important hygiene decisions.

## Digital Technologies

**Building and testing**

**Lesson 1 — Build the core function**
- Make the solution work before making it look impressive.
- Implement essential inputs, sequence/algorithm and intended output.

**Lesson 2 — Test systematically**
- Record expected result, actual result, whether it worked and what needs changing.

**Lesson 3 — Debug**
- Identify/correct sequencing errors, missing instructions, unexpected
  outputs and input problems.

**Lesson 4 — Peer testing**
- A peer uses the product without creator explanation.
- Record what worked, where the user was confused and what should improve.
