# Week 8 — 30 November–4 December 2026

## Weekly purpose

- Return decisively from assessment to learning.
- Emphasise creative/responsive English, probability, richer HASS
  application, informed hygiene choices and Digital Technologies extension.

## English — Writing

**Revision, experimentation and publishing**

**Lesson 1 — What revision actually means**
- Distinguish revising ideas/structure, editing sentences and proofreading surface errors.
- Revise an earlier non-assessment piece.

**Lesson 2 — Changing perspective**
- Perspective changes what is noticed, known, interpreted and how language is used.
- Rewrite a familiar scene from another character’s point of view.

**Lesson 3 — Manipulating setting**
- Use language choices to change mood.
- Write the same basic event in two contrasting settings or moods.

**Lesson 4 — Experimenting with structure**
- Explore opening in the middle of action, flashback, circular ending or withheld information.
- Keep experimentation purposeful.

**Lesson 5 — Publish or share**
- Select a piece worth refining for an authentic audience; do not reassess for reports.

## English — Reading

**Literature after assessment**

**Lesson 1 — Multiple interpretations**
- Compare reasonable interpretations of a text.

**Lesson 2 — Theme**
- Track how a theme develops across a complete story.

**Lesson 3 — Figurative and precise language**
- Examine how language contributes to imagery and mood.

**Lesson 4 — Perspective**
- Consider whose perspective dominates and whose is absent.

**Lesson 5 — Literature discussion**
- Support opinions with specific textual evidence.

## Mathematics

**Chance and probability: language and likelihood**

**Lesson 1 — Chance events**
- Classify events using impossible, unlikely, likely, certain and equally likely where appropriate.
- Require justification.

**Lesson 2 — Comparing likelihood**
- Compare two events and justify which is more likely using possible outcomes rather than personal intuition alone.

**Lesson 3 — Sample spaces**
- Identify all possible outcomes in simple coin, dice, spinner and counter contexts.

**Lesson 4 — Fairness**
- Investigate what makes a simple game fair or unfair.

**Lesson 5 — Design a chance game**
- Create a simple chance game and predict possible outcomes, likely/unlikely outcomes and fairness.

## HASS

**Applied resource-allocation challenge**

**Lesson 1 — A realistic decision**
- Use a larger scenario with limited resources and competing priorities such as a community area, school budget or event services.
- Groups propose an allocation.

**Lesson 2 — Defending a decision**
- Explain priorities, resource limits, trade-offs, who benefits and what was not chosen.

**Lesson 3 — Comparing solutions**
- Compare group proposals; do not identify a single “winner” where multiple allocations are defensible.

## Health

**Hygiene products and informed choices**

**Lesson 1 — What does the product actually do?**
- Examine common age-appropriate hygiene products and distinguish purpose, instructions, claims and advertising.

**Lesson 2 — Making informed choices**
- Consider need, effectiveness, cost, instructions, personal preference and waste.
- Do not imply expensive products are automatically superior.
- Analyse fictional purchasing decisions.

## Digital Technologies

**Extension project**

**Lesson 1 — Review and redesign**
- Identify one worthwhile improvement to the assessed solution.

**Lesson 2 — Add complexity**
- Depending on capability, add choices, branching, scoring, useful feedback, input or navigation.

**Lesson 3 — Implement**
- Build the selected extension.

**Lesson 4 — Test**
- Use the systematic testing routine established earlier.
