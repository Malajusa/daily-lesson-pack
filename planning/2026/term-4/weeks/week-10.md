# Week 10 — 14–17 December 2026

## Weekly purpose

- Finish the year with meaningful learning rather than filler.
- Consolidate important skills, apply learning, reflect on growth,
  complete/share worthwhile products and remain flexible for a four-day final week.

## English — Writing

**Reflection, publication and choice**

**Lesson 1 — Looking back at growth**
- Compare an earlier piece with more recent work.
- Identify one clear improvement, one developing skill and evidence for each judgement.

**Lesson 2 — Revision challenge**
- Improve an older non-assessment piece using structure, cohesion, character, sentence craft and vocabulary.

**Lesson 3 — Student-selected writing**
- Choose a form or narrative idea to pursue; prioritise independence rather than a common prompt.

**Lesson 4 — Editing and publication**
- Separate revision, editing and proofreading.

**Lesson 5 — Sharing**
- Use a low-pressure authentic audience such as a partner, small group, anthology, display or read-aloud.

## English — Reading

**Reading for understanding and enjoyment**

**Lesson 1 — Student-selected text**
- Apply comprehension strategies independently.

**Lesson 2 — Recommend a text**
- Justify a recommendation using specific features.

**Lesson 3 — Compare texts**
- Compare character, theme, setting or author craft.

**Lesson 4 — Reading challenge**
- Use a short unfamiliar text with higher-level inference and vocabulary.

**Lesson 5 — Reading reflection**
- Identify strategies that now help when a text becomes difficult.

## Mathematics

**Statistics, data and integrated problem solving**

**Lesson 1 — Reading data displays**
- Year 4: focus on appropriate pictographs/column graphs; Year 5: include line graphs where appropriate.
- Ask questions requiring more than locating one value.

**Lesson 2 — What does the graph actually show?**
- Distinguish supported conclusions from unsupported claims or misleading interpretations.

**Lesson 3 — Representing data**
- Provide a data set and ask students to select/construct an appropriate representation and justify the choice.

**Lesson 4 — Integrated problem solving**
- Use a rich task combining several areas of mathematics from the year.
- Students select strategies, show working and communicate conclusions.

**Lesson 5 — Mathematical challenge**
- Use collaborative high-interest problems with multiple solutions, optimisation, logic, spatial reasoning or data rather than another test.

## HASS

**Applying the big idea**

**Lesson 1 — Return to the inquiry question**
- Revisit “Why can’t people always have everything they want?” using resources, scarcity, choice, trade-offs and consequences.
- Give a fuller reflective answer; do not treat this as reassessment.

**Lesson 2 — Class resource challenge**
- Use a fictional limited resource and competing possible uses.
- Groups propose and justify allocations.

**Lesson 3 — Reflection**
- Identify one Economics idea that changed or refined how students think about everyday decisions.

## Health

**Hygiene consolidation and independence**

**Lesson 1 — Build a realistic routine**
- Create a balanced fictional hygiene routine across morning, school, sport, meals and evening.
- Justify major choices.

**Lesson 2 — Myth or sensible advice?**
- Present age-appropriate hygiene claims and decide whether each is sensible, exaggerated or needs more information.
- Emphasise reasoning rather than slogans.

**Lesson 3 — Personal responsibility and shared spaces**
- Finish with the idea that hygiene has personal benefits and effects on shared environments/other people.
- Do not require students to disclose personal hygiene habits.

## Digital Technologies

**Complete, share and reflect**

**Lesson 1 — Final build**
- Complete only the highest-value remaining improvements.

**Lesson 2 — Final testing**
- Check from a user’s perspective.

**Lesson 3 — Showcase**
- Demonstrate what the solution does, how users interact, one problem encountered and how it was solved.

**Lesson 4 — Reflection**
- Explain what would be done differently if rebuilding from the beginning.

## Assessment and evidence

- No new summative assessment. Use work for reflection, transition information and instructional insight only.

## Preparation and resources

- Earlier non-assessment writing
- Student-selected reading options
- Year-appropriate data displays/datasets
- Rich maths challenge tasks
- HASS resource challenge
- Hygiene claim cards
- Digital showcase/testing arrangements

## End-of-week checkpoint

**English**
- Assessed narrative evidence remains secured; students continue authentic reading/writing

**Mathematics**
- Geometry evidence secured by Week 7; probability/statistics learning extends curriculum

**HASS**
- Students can explain/apply the main Economics ideas

**Health**
- Students reason about hygiene decisions across real-life contexts

**Digital Technologies**
- Students can extend, test, present and reflect on a functional digital solution

## Week constraint

Week 10 has only four official term days. Level C should prioritise the
highest-value lessons once the actual timetable and end-of-year events
are known; the B sequence is an ordered menu, not a requirement to force
five full lessons into four days.
