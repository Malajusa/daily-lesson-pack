# Week 1 — 12–16 October 2026

## Weekly purpose

- Establish the foundations for each Term 4 unit before the heavier
  teaching load of Week 2 and the reduced instructional time caused by
  swimming in Weeks 3–4.

- Prioritise shared language and concepts, explicit modelling,
  identification of prior knowledge and misconceptions, short formative
  work and routines that can continue during disrupted weeks.

## English — Writing

**Narrative: structure, orientation, character and setting**

**Lesson 1 — What makes a narrative work?**

**Focus:** Narrative structure

**Key teaching points**

- Narratives are more than a sequence of events.
- Strong narratives usually contain an orientation, complication, rising
  action, climax and resolution.
- Each part has a purpose.

**Student task/output**

- Read or revisit a short model narrative.
- Identify and label its major structural components.
- Explain what would happen if one component were removed.

**Essential resources**

- High-quality model narrative
- Narrative structure organiser

**Lesson 2 — Effective openings**

**Focus:** Orientating the reader efficiently

**Key teaching points**

- Introduce enough information for the reader to understand the
  situation without over-explaining.
- Establish character, setting and situation.
- Avoid beginning with a long list of background facts.

**Student task/output**

- Compare several narrative openings.
- Identify which establish the story most effectively and why.
- Write two alternative openings for the same scenario.

**Essential resources**

- Strong and weak opening examples
- Short writing prompts

**Lesson 3 — Building characters**

**Focus:** Revealing character through action, dialogue, thoughts and
description

**Key teaching points**

- Character traits do not need to be stated directly.
- What a character wants can drive the story.
- Actions and dialogue provide evidence about personality.

**Student task/output**

- Infer traits from a model passage.
- Develop a character with a clear goal or motivation.
- Write a short scene that reveals a trait without naming it.

**Lesson 4 — Creating setting**

**Focus:** Using setting purposefully rather than simply describing a
place

**Key teaching points**

- Setting can influence mood, character behaviour and events.
- Selective sensory detail is generally stronger than describing
  everything.
- Precise nouns and verbs often improve description more than excessive
  adjectives.

**Student task/output**

- Examine how a model text creates atmosphere.
- Rewrite a neutral setting to create a particular mood.
- Establish a setting that could support a future complication.

**Lesson 5 — Bringing the opening together**

**Focus:** Applying Week 1 learning

**Student task/output**

- Independently write the opening section of a narrative that
  establishes a character and setting, gives the character a motivation
  or situation, and prepares the reader for a possible complication.

## English — Reading

**Narrative comprehension and author choices**

**Lesson 1 — Literal and inferred information**

**Key teaching points**

- Distinguish information stated directly from information readers must
  infer.
- Require evidence to support an inference.

**Student task/output**

- Complete evidence-based responses about character or situation.

**Lesson 2 — Character motivation**

**Key teaching points**

- Identify what a character wants, what prevents them getting it and how
  motivation affects decisions.

**Student task/output**

- Link reading analysis to the character work in writing.

**Lesson 3 — Vocabulary in context**

**Key teaching points**

- Use surrounding information to infer word meaning.
- Examine word parts where useful.
- Distinguish approximate meaning from precise meaning.
- Test inferred meanings against the sentence.

**Lesson 4 — Important versus incidental detail**

**Key teaching points**

- Identify details that move the narrative forward, reveal character or
  develop setting/mood.
- Distinguish essential details from interesting but incidental details.

**Lesson 5 — Author choices**

**Key teaching points**

- Ask why an author may have written a passage in a particular way.
- Focus on accessible choices such as word choice, sentence length,
  dialogue, withholding information and description.

**Student task/output**

- Explain the effect of one or two author choices.

## Mathematics

**2D shapes: combining and splitting**

**Lesson 1 — Properties and classification**

**Focus:** Revisiting the language needed for the unit

**Key teaching points**

- Classify shapes using properties rather than appearance.
- Use sides, vertices, parallel/perpendicular lines, equal sides and
  angle language where relevant.

**Student task/output**

- Sort and justify classifications.

**Lesson 2 — Shapes within shapes**

**Focus:** Identifying component shapes inside composite figures

**Key teaching points**

- Locate familiar shapes within larger figures.
- Recognise that the same composite figure can sometimes be partitioned
  in different ways.

**Essential resources**

- Printed composite figures
- Manipulatives
- Grid paper

**Lesson 3 — Combining shapes**

**Key teaching points**

- Use known 2D shapes to construct larger shapes, specified figures and
  multiple solutions to the same problem.

**Student task/output**

- Explain which shapes were combined and how the new figure meets the
  conditions.

**Lesson 4 — Splitting composite shapes**

**Key teaching points**

- Partition complex figures into simpler known shapes.
- Progress from obvious partitions to multiple possible partitions and
  student-selected dividing lines.

**Lesson 5 — Reasoning and application**

**Key teaching points**

- Solve unfamiliar composing/decomposing problems.
- Include more than one valid solution, explanations and correction of
  incorrect solutions.

## HASS

**Choices, needs and wants**

**Lesson 1 — Needs and wants**

**Focus:** Establishing the distinction

**Key teaching points**

- Generate, classify and discuss examples, including ambiguous cases.
- Recognise that context can affect classification.
- Avoid reducing the idea to “needs are good, wants are bad”.

**Lesson 2 — Why do people make choices?**

**Key teaching points**

- People have many needs and wants but limited resources, time and
  money.

**Student task/output**

- For each scenario: identify available choices, select one, explain
  influences on the decision and identify what was given up.

## Health

**Hygiene: foundations**

**Lesson 1 — What is hygiene?**

**Key teaching points**

- Explore personal hygiene, environmental hygiene and why hygiene
  matters.
- Connect hygiene with health, comfort and interactions with other
  people.

**Student task/output**

- Sort examples into personal hygiene, environmental hygiene or both.

**Lesson 2 — Hygiene routines and misconceptions**

**Key teaching points**

- Analyse washing hands, showering, changing clothes, brushing teeth,
  cleaning shared surfaces and respiratory hygiene.
- Focus on why routines exist rather than simply listing rules.

**Student task/output**

- Explain several hygiene behaviours using cause-and-effect language.

## Digital Technologies

**Digital systems**

**Lesson 1 — What is a digital system?**

**Key teaching points**

- Begin with familiar examples rather than abstract definitions.
- Ask what the system does, what information goes in, what happens to it
  and what comes out.

**Student task/output**

- Investigate laptops, tablets, interactive panels, consoles, printers,
  cameras or similar systems.

**Lesson 2 — Parts working together**

**Key teaching points**

- Introduce input, processing, output, hardware and software at an
  appropriate level.

**Student task/output**

- Select a familiar digital system and create a simple labelled
  explanation of how its parts work together.
