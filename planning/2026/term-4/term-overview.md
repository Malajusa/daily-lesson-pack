# Level A — Term Overview

This section carries the curriculum, sequencing and assessment layer.
Weekly overviews in Level B remain timetable-agnostic; the actual Room
11 timetable is applied only in Level C.

## Term parameters and constraints

**Term dates:** Monday 12 October to Thursday 17 December 2026 (WA
public-school term dates; school development days may alter local
attendance days).

**English — Writing:** Narrative.

**English — Reading:** Literature, comprehension and author craft;
HASS-linked texts used where they genuinely strengthen knowledge without
replacing the English reading program.

**Mathematics:** Geometry through Week 7, followed by probability and
statistics/application in Weeks 8–10.

**HASS:** Year 5 Economics and Business: wants, resources, scarcity,
choices and trade-offs. Year 4 students share inquiry and
decision-making contexts; Economics and Business formally commences in
Year 5.

**Health:** Hygiene, including hand, oral, body, swimming/shared-space,
food and respiratory hygiene, product choices and practical
decision-making.

**Digital Technologies:** Digital systems, data representation,
privacy/security, algorithms, digital implementation, testing and
evaluation.

**Science:** Specialist subject — excluded from this classroom planning
document.

**Swimming:** Weeks 3 and 4; approximately two hours of classroom
working time lost each day. These weeks are deliberately reduced-load.

**Assessment deadline:** All reportable assessment evidence complete by
the end of Week 7.

**Narrative assessment:** Fixed in Week 6.

**Weeks 8–10:** Post-assessment curriculum learning, application,
extension, intervention and consolidation — not filler.

## Curriculum mapping

Codes are included where the current Western Australian Curriculum
publishes a code. For revised 2026 HASS and Digital Technologies
content, the current scope-and-sequence material is presented primarily
as mandated content descriptions; this document does not invent codes
where none are displayed in the source used.

### English

**Year 4 — WA4ELYC1:** Plan, create, edit and publish written and
multimodal imaginative, informative and persuasive texts, including
linked ideas in paragraphs, complex sentences, appropriate tense,
vocabulary, spelling and punctuation.

**Year 4 — WA4ELYA1:** Identify characteristic features used in
imaginative, informative and persuasive texts to meet the purpose of the
text.

**Year 5 — WA5ELYC1:** Plan, create, edit and publish written and
multimodal texts for imaginative, informative and persuasive purposes,
developing ideas with appropriate text structure, connectives,
vocabulary and punctuation, including dialogue punctuation.

**Year 5 — WA5ELICR1:** Create and edit literary texts, experimenting
with figurative language, storylines, characters and settings from texts
students have experienced.

**Year 5 — WA5ELYA1:** Explain characteristic features used in
imaginative, informative and persuasive texts to meet the purpose of the
text.

Term application: narrative structure, character, setting, complication,
cohesion, tension, dialogue, climax/resolution, editing and author-craft
analysis; reading work develops literal/inferred meaning and
evidence-based interpretation.

### Mathematics

**Year 4**

**WA4MMGTW1:** Explore, visualise, describe and create 2D shapes that
result from combining or splitting familiar shapes.

**WA4MMGTW4:** Indirectly compare angles and identify them as equal to,
greater than or less than a right angle.

**WA4MMGTW5:** Create or interpret grid maps, describe positions and
pathways, and explore scales and legends.

**WA4MMGTH1:** Connect 3D objects to their 2D representations and
visualise/describe key features that cannot be seen.

**WA4MPSP1:** Order the likelihood of everyday chance events and
identify when events are not affected by previous events.

**WA4MPSP2:** Predict outcomes of unequally likely repeated chance
experiments; conduct experiments, describe variation and compare with
prediction.

**WA4MPSS1:** Describe and interpret real-life data represented in
many-to-one pictographs and column graphs.

**Year 5**

**WA5MMGTW1:** Explore line and rotational symmetry in 2D shapes.

**WA5MMGTW5:** Estimate, measure and construct angles in degrees using a
protractor; classify acute, right, obtuse, reflex and straight angles.

**WA5MMGTW6:** Use directional language, grid references and grid
coordinates to describe positions and pathways.

**WA5MMGTH1:** Visualise and connect 3D objects to their nets and build
objects from their nets.

**WA5MPSP1:** Compare everyday chance events, grouping outcomes as
equally likely or not equally likely.

**WA5MPSP2:** Conduct repeated chance experiments with equally likely
outcomes; represent results as fractions, compare results and discuss
variation.

**WA5MPSS1:** Describe and interpret line graphs showing how real-life
continuous data changes over time.

Mixed-year note: Week 2 symmetry is new mandated content for Year 5 and
consolidation/extension for Year 4. Week 10 statistics should
differentiate: Year 4 focuses on pictographs/column graphs while Year 5
includes line graphs.

### HASS — Economics and Business

Economics and Business commences formally in Year 5. Year 5 Term 4
knowledge focuses on wants, resources and choices:

- The difference between needs and wants, and how they may differ
  between individuals.

- Resources can be natural, human or capital and are used to make goods
  and services to satisfy needs and wants.

- Due to scarcity, choices need to be made about how limited resources
  are used.

- Alternative uses of resources create trade-offs and consequences in
  decision-making.

- Students use HASS inquiry processes to investigate, analyse, evaluate,
  communicate and reflect.

Year 4 students may participate in the same contexts and inquiry
activities, but Year 5 Economics knowledge should not be misrepresented
as mandated Year 4 Economics and Business content.

### Health — Hygiene

**WA4HEHPH1:** Strategies that promote a healthy lifestyle.

**WA4HEHPH2:** Ways in which health information can influence health
decisions and behaviours.

**WA4HEHPP2:** Changes associated with puberty.

**WA4HEHPP3:** Strategies to manage changes associated with puberty.

**WA5HEHPH1:** Strategies that promote a safe, healthy lifestyle.

**WA5HEHPH2:** Health messages that support and maintain an individual’s
health, safety and wellbeing.

**WA5HEHPP2:** Strategies to manage physical, mental/emotional and
social changes associated with puberty.

Term application: personal hygiene is taught through practical health
decision-making, with puberty-related content limited to age-appropriate
changes in hygiene needs unless broader puberty teaching is separately
planned.

### Digital Technologies — 2026 curriculum

The revised Digital Technologies curriculum is implemented in 2026.
Relevant Year 4 and Year 5 mandated content used in this term includes:

**Year 4**

- Digital systems, including peripheral devices, are used to transfer
  and store different types of data.
- Data of the same type can be represented in different ways depending
  on purpose.
- Personal data shared and stored online can pose risks.
- Represent algorithms involving decisions (branching) and repetition
  using flow charts.
- Implement algorithms in a visual programming environment to include
  branching and repetition.
- Evaluate the designed solution using given criteria, including
  selected resources and decision-making.

**Year 5**

- Digital systems have main internal components that perform particular
  functions to achieve a purpose.
- Data of all types, including text, numbers, sound and images, are
  represented using codes.
- Personal data can be used to create a permanent digital footprint.
- Design algorithms in plain English and/or flow charts involving user
  input, variables and control structures (sequence, decisions and
  repetition).
- Implement algorithms in a visual programming environment involving
  variables and control structures with user input.
- Evaluate the designed solution using given criteria, considering
  competing factors and processes.
